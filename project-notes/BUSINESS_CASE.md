## 1. The idea

  Hockey Gear Near You is a local marketplace that mainly has a target customer of parents that need to find affordable options for hockey gear for their kids that are playing hockey. A parent trying to find all of the needed gear for their kids might go to multiple stores and see that the prices are just far too expensive.  They might then try to look on Facebook marketplace for some cheaper options but then realize that while they are showing the gear to their kid to see if they like it enough to buy it someone else has already purchased it in that time. While at the same time families who have kids who no longer play the sport may just have gear sitting around their house that they are looking to sell.
  Hockey Gear Near You would bring local buyers and sellers together. Each listing would show the main details on the piece of equipment like the price, size, condition, and location of where you would pick it up. When a parent finds a piece of equipment that they think their kid would like, there is an option to reserve so that it won’t be sold to somebody else while they are still deciding if they would like to purchase it. Families could browse and post listings for free and the business would earn a small fee from the seller when a sale is actually made. 


## 2. Where IT creates value

  Hockey Gear Near You uses information technology to improve marketing and sales. Imagine a parent trying to sell their kids old goalie gear that they no longer use because they grew out of it or no longer play the sport. They might post it on Facebook marketplace or some other kind of buy and sell group, where their post can get buried in among newer posts. This new website would give the seller another distribution channel. When a nearby parent goes on the website and searches for goalie gear, the website will show them all of the goalie gear on there whether it was posted one day ago or one month ago. This helps the seller reach someone local who is already looking for that type of gear.
  When the buyer opens that listing, they can see the price, condition, size, and the location of where it would need to be picked up. This is an example of differentiation because the site organizes listings around details that matter to the buyers. If the listing says that the goalie pads are $100 the buyer can decide if it is in their price range or not. If it is they can then reserve it for pickup. The website helps sales move forward by listing these important details and giving the buyer a clear next step.

## 3. Network effects

  Hockey Gear Near You is a two-sided market. The two sides to this market are the buyers, who are the local families looking to find affordable gear for their kids. The other side is the sellers, who are the local families that are looking to gid rid of old gear that is no longer of use to their kids. As this network grows, both the buyers and sellers would benefit. With more local families looking to sell their gear the buyers gain more affordable options to look for equipment for their kids so that they can continue to play the sport. At the same time, having more nearby buyers will improve the sellers chances of selling their equipment that is no longer in use. 
  To get this started, I would approach a minor hockey association and tell them about my network that I am trying to get running. I would try to get the coaches of local teams to pass on the details of my network to the families of the players to look for people who currently have old gear sitting around that their child has outgrown already and show them that there is a new option specifically for hockey where they can post those outgrown pieces of equipment and try to clear up some space around their house and make a little bit of extra money to go along with it.
  The page that shows this on my prototype is the explore page. When you search for goalie gear for example, when you switch the search from one association to three, the same search goes from one listing to two. This extra match means that now there is another option available for the buyer to look into before they purchase a piece of equipment.

## 4. Can it be copied? (the moat question)

  With the same AI tools I used, a competitor could recreate the searchable listings and the demo reservation that I have on my network in a weekend. One resource that would be an advantage to me is having a real relationship with the hockey association. Having this relationship would be valuable to the network because it gives me a large access to local hockey families all at once. If a competitor tries to compete with my network but has zero connections to hockey families before starting, then it would take much longer to get their network out to all the people who will be using it.  This relationship with the hockey association itself isn’t rare and if the association is willing to promote multiple markets, then the relationship would be at the same use to competitors. At the same time, even if the competitors had got the help of the association to introduce them to the same local families, they would still have to persuade those families to try out their new marketplace. This is where it might take a much longer time for the competitor to build up enough listings and active buyers in the same town which makes it hard to imitate. Lastly, this relationship could be substitutable if a competitor was able to get a good relationship with a local rink. If they had this relationship, they could try to get the owners of the rink to put up ads all around the ice and walls in the building promoting their network.
  Another resource to my advantage would be a steady supply of used gear from returning sellers. This would be valuable to my network because these consistent regular listings across different sizes and equipment would give a wide variety to buyers who need a new piece of equipment. This resource is rare in the sense that buyers would be able to find the gear they are looking for at local sports stores, but those places would also be much more expensive. This could be imitable if the competitor had a good number of connections to people like family and friends who have old gear sitting around that they would be willing to sell. This relationship could be substitutable for the same reason as why it could be imitable, if the competitor has a large number of family and friends, they wouldn’t need the other hockey families that I would have from the returning sellers. 
  For a competitor with a bigger budget than me to copy me they would need to recruit local buyers and sellers that have a useful selection of gear. If they are starting with zero connections, I would estimate this to take about a full hockey season because they would need to build those connections with the locals before trying to get them to try out their marketplace. My advantage of returning sellers would get stronger over time as the kids get older, they would be growing out of new sizes of gear all the time giving my marketplace a variety of equipment for buyers to choose from.

## 5. Cloud and operations

  As I explained in the “What a real version would need” table in my project log, for this to run as a real marketplace the sellers would need user accounts so that they can sign in and edit their listings and make sure that they’ve uploaded the correct photos of the piece of equipment and they would also need user accounts so that they’re able to regularly check on their listed items to see if any of them have been reserved or not. A part that I would buy as cloud services would be a hosted sign-in service which is a SaaS. This service would take care of signing users in while my site would still have to check that each seller can only edit their own personal listings. If this service went down but the rest of the site was still working, then buyers could still browse listings, but sellers would have to wait to sign in again to make any changes. If prices went up, keeping listings linked to each seller’s account ID would help keep track of who owns each listing if sign-in providers were switched.

## 6. Risks and ethics

  One of the risks that customers should be aware of in this website is pickup privacy. My prototype for this website shows a general pickup area, like Antigonish, so that buyers can tell how close the gear is to them. If a real market were to display the seller’s home address publicly in the details about the gear they are posting, anyone browsing the site would then have access to where that family lives. To make sure that this doesn’t happen I would just keep the town visible and then once the buyer and seller arrange a pickup the seller can provide the buyer with the address privately. 
  Another risk with this is a misleading condition. The seller could advertise a piece of equipment that has no problems when it really does have a broken piece on it. What I would do to reduce this risk is give the buyers a way to report a listing if they find out that it isn’t advertised correctly on the website.
